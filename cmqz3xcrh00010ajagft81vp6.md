On **June 27, 2026**, something special happened at the **American Center Kampala.**

After months of planning, countless emails, sponsor outreach, late-night edits, and moments of uncertainty, we successfully hosted the **Build with AI Makerere Hackathon** under GDG on Campus at Makerere University in partnership with Google Build with AI and Major League Hacking (MLH).

Seeing more than 60 students walk through the doors with laptops, excitement, and ambitious ideas made every challenge worthwhile.

This wasn't just another event.

It was my first time organizing a hackathon of this scale on my own, and it taught me lessons that no tutorial, course, or book could have.

## Why We Organized the Hackathon

Artificial Intelligence has become one of the most important technologies of our generation. Many students attend talks about AI, but very few get the opportunity actually to build with it.

Our goal was simple:

> Give students a place where they could move from learning AI to creating real AI-powered solutions.

Using Gemini, Google AI Studio, Google Cloud, Cursor, and other modern AI tools, participants spent the day showcasing solutions that addressed real-world problems.

## The Numbers

Some of the highlights included:

*   250 registrations
    
*   Over 60 in-person participants
    
*   Students from multiple universities across Uganda
    
*   Three winning teams
    
*   Multiple ecosystem partners are supporting the event
    

Seeing registrations fill so quickly was a reminder that there is an incredible appetite for AI education and innovation among students in Uganda.

## Building the Event Before the Event

One thing I learned is that successful hackathons don't begin on hackathon day.

They begin months earlier.

Before participants even arrived, we organized:

*   Build with AI workshops
    
*   Google AI Studio training
    
*   Cursor workshop
    
*   Launch and information sessions
    
*   Community discussions
    
*   Technical mentorship
    

These sessions helped participants arrive prepared rather than overwhelmed. Looking back, I believe these pre-events contributed significantly to the quality of projects we received.

## Finding Partners Was One of the Hardest Parts

Perhaps the biggest lesson was that sponsorship doesn't happen overnight.

Most partnerships came after sending numerous emails, following up, explaining the vision repeatedly, and sometimes simply waiting.

Over time, we were fortunate to receive support from:

*   Google Build with AI
    
*   Major League Hacking
    
*   Cursor
    
*   Featherless AI
    
*   GitHub
    
*   American Center Kampala
    

Each partnership added something unique. Some provided prizes. Others provided technical resources. Some opened doors that would otherwise have remained closed.

This reminded me that partnerships are built on relationships rather than requests.

## The Challenges No One Sees

From the outside, people usually only see the photos. They rarely see the weeks behind them.

Some of the challenges included:

*   Trying to schedule around university examination periods.
    
*   Preparing multiple event platforms, including Devpost and MLH.
    
*   Designing marketing materials.
    
*   Finding judges.
    
*   Following up with sponsors.
    
*   Managing logistics with a limited budget.
    
*   Last-minute participant communication.
    

There were many moments when it felt overwhelming.

> Yet each challenge forced me to become a better organizer.

## Leadership Means Solving Problems

One realization stood out throughout the process. Community leadership isn't about having all the answers. It's about continuing to solve problems even when new ones appear every day.

<mark class="bg-yellow-200 dark:bg-yellow-500/30">Every email.</mark>

<mark class="bg-yellow-200 dark:bg-yellow-500/30">Every sponsor conversation.</mark>

<mark class="bg-yellow-200 dark:bg-yellow-500/30">Every design revision.</mark>

<mark class="bg-yellow-200 dark:bg-yellow-500/30">Every schedule adjustment.</mark>

Each one became part of building something bigger than myself.

## The Projects

The quality of submissions genuinely impressed me. The winning teams demonstrated that students can create meaningful AI solutions when given access to the right tools and mentorship.

Among the winning projects were:

🥇 **SANI AI**

An AI-powered health triage assistant and epidemic sentinel designed for Uganda.

🥈 **Mobile Money Scam Checker Uganda**

Helping users identify and avoid mobile money fraud.

🥉 **Smart Invoice Reader**

An AI-powered invoice management solution for African businesses.

These projects showed that AI is becoming increasingly practical for solving local challenges.

## Lessons I'll Carry Forward

If I had to summarize everything into a few lessons, they would be these:

*   **Start planning earlier than you think you need to.**
    

The earlier sponsorship, venue planning, and marketing begin, the more flexibility you have later.

*   **Relationships matter more than resources.**
    

Many opportunities came because people believed in the vision.

*   **Communication is everything.**
    

Keeping participants informed reduced confusion and built trust.

*   **Community comes before the event itself.**
    

The workshops leading up to the hackathon were just as important as the hackathon.

*   **You don't need to know everything to lead.**
    

You simply need to be willing to learn quickly and keep moving forward.

## Gratitude

This event would not have been possible without the support of our participants, volunteers, mentors, judges, sponsors, and partners.

Thank you to **Google Build with AI, Major League Hacking, Cursor, Featherless AI, GitHub, the American Center Kampala**, and everyone who believed in this vision.

Most importantly, thank you to **every student** who chose to spend their day building instead of simply watching technology evolve.

## Looking Ahead

The Build with AI Makerere Hackathon was never meant to be the finish line.

It is the beginning.

The excitement, creativity, and talent we witnessed reminded me that Uganda has an incredible generation of builders ready to shape the future of AI.

As an organizer, I'm leaving this experience with more confidence, stronger partnerships, and a deeper appreciation for what it takes to build thriving developer communities.

And this is only the beginning.
