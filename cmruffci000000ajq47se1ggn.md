On July 18, 2026, we hosted the [Build with Gemma Hackathon at Makerere University](https://www.kaggle.com/t/4aaae7ecfd56738e8622e55f8e3ddfbb), bringing together over 30 students and developers to build AI-powered solutions using Google's open Gemma models.

The event concluded with 11 project submissions, three winning teams, and countless lessons learned. But what most people saw was only the final day.

What they didn't see was the journey that led us there.

## The Idea

The opportunity started when the Build with Gemma team opened applications for community-led hackathons.

As GDG on Campus Makerere, we saw it as a chance to give students practical experience building with AI while showcasing what our community could accomplish.

The vision was simple:

> Create a space where students could learn, experiment, and build meaningful AI solutions in a single day.

## The First Challenge: Planning

Like many community events, the first challenge wasn't technology but rather logistics.

We had to:

*   Define the event format
    
*   Create a Kaggle competition
    
*   Prepare rules and judging criteria
    
*   Recruit mentors and judges
    
*   Find a venue
    
*   Promote the event
    
*   Manage registrations
    

Every decision affected another part of the event.

## Working Through Uncertainty

There were moments when things didn't go as planned.

Registration numbers moved slower than expected.

We adjusted our messaging.

We organized a [Build with Gemma workshop](https://youtu.be/foGVq0nQhMw?si=woljtcca3d-WCNwA) before the hackathon to help participants understand the technology and feel confident enough to compete.

That workshop became one of the most important decisions we made.

It transformed curiosity into commitment.

## Building Partnerships

One of the biggest lessons from organizing community events is that you rarely do it alone.

We worked with:

*   The Build with Gemma team
    
*   Kaggle
    
*   Google Developer Groups
    
*   MIIC Hub
    
*   Zindi
    
*   Mentors and volunteers
    

Every partner contributed something important, whether it was venue support, technical guidance, or resources for participants.

## Event Day

On July 18, participants arrived at MIIC Hub ready to build.

The room quickly filled with discussions, brainstorming sessions, debugging, and demonstrations. Throughout the day, participants explored how Gemma could be applied to:

*   Education
    
*   Healthcare
    
*   Community impact
    
*   Productivity
    
*   Local language solutions
    

The energy in the room reminded us why hackathons matter.

They're not just competitions.

They're environments where people learn faster together than they would alone.

## The Results

The event welcomed more than 30 participants and produced 11 project submissions.

Our winners were:

### 🥇 Kasasiro Coin: Turn Rubbish into Rewards

Built by Bahati Brenda Kizito

A creative solution that incentivizes responsible waste management through rewards.

### 🥈 GemaCard: Digitizing Uganda's Child Immunization Records with Gemma 4

Built by Bronia Boonabaana

A project focused on improving access to child healthcare records through digital transformation.

### 🥉 MedPilot AI

Built by Ian Noah

An AI-powered healthcare solution designed to improve access to medical information and support.

## What I Learned

A few lessons stood out:

### Start community engagement early

People don't register because a hackathon exists.

They register because they understand why it matters.

### Workshops increase participation

The Build with Gemma workshop gave participants confidence and helped convert interest into action.

### Partnerships are everything

Strong community events are built through collaboration.

### Impact isn't measured only by winners

The biggest success wasn't the prize pool.

It was seeing participants learn, build, and submit projects they were proud of.

## Looking Ahead

The Build with Gemma Hackathon was more than a one-day event.

It was proof that student communities can create meaningful opportunities for learning and innovation.

As we continue building GDG on Campus Makerere, we're excited to organize more workshops, hackathons, and community initiatives that help students move from learning about technology to creating with it.

To everyone who participated, mentored, volunteered, judged, and supported the event:

Thank you.

We couldn't have done it without you.
